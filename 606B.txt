Answer
The boxplots suggest a small difference in weight between students who are physically active at least three days per week and those who are not. Students in the "yes" group appear to have a slightly higher median weight.
This result may be surprising because many people expect more active students to weigh less. However, physically active students may have greater muscle mass, which contributes to a higher body weight even if they are healthier overall.

Construct and interpret a confidence interval for the difference in mean weights.

ci_weight <- yrbss %>%
 drop_na(weight, physical_3plus) %>%
 specify(weight ~ physical_3plus) %>%
 generate(reps = 1000, type = "bootstrap") %>%
 calculate(stat = "diff in means",
          order = c("yes","no")) %>%
 get_confidence_interval(level = .95)

Answer
The 90% confidence interval is narrower than the 95% confidence interval.
This occurs because lower confidence requires capturing less of the sampling distribution, producing a smaller margin of error.

Conduct a hypothesis test evaluating whether average height differs by activity level.
obs_diff_height <- yrbss %>%
 drop_na(height, physical_3plus) %>%
 specify(height ~ physical_3plus) %>%
 calculate(stat = "diff in means",
          order = c("yes","no"))

null_height <- yrbss %>%
 drop_na(height, physical_3plus) %>%
 specify(height ~ physical_3plus) %>%
 hypothesize(null = "independence") %>%
 generate(reps = 1000, type = "permute") %>%
 calculate(stat = "diff in means",
          order = c("yes","no"))

null_height %>%
 get_p_value(obs_stat = obs_diff_height,
            direction = "two_sided")
If the p-value is below 0.05, reject the null hypothesis and conclude there is evidence that average height differs between the activity groups.
Otherwise, fail to reject the null hypothesis.
